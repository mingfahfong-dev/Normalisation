How CCLAS normalises an XRF result

  1. The instrument measures the fused bead and gives the raw X value for each analyte (XFE, XSIO2 …).
  2. It corrects to a dry basis with H2OFCT = 100 ÷ (100 − H2O) (H2O is the residual moisture of the pulp), and for the process weight with the WT Factor (1 for process P).
  3. It adds up the oxides: OXIDES = XFe×1.4297 + XP×2.2914 + XSiO2 + XAl2O3 + XTiO2 + XCaO + XMn×1.3883 + XMgO + XK2O (S and Na2O are not included). OXTRUE = OXIDES × WT × H2OFCT, and the mass balance MB = OXTRUE + LOI.
  4. It scales the oxides so that oxides + LOI = 99.95, holding LOI fixed: NF = (99.95 − LOI) ÷ OXTRUE.
  5. The reported result = X × WT × H2OFCT × NF. AVG and cargo rows are the averages of their DUPs, so they have no X values and show NF = 1.000. Their MB is the average of the DUPs' MB.

MB methodDefault, best for every row

Un-normalised = Reported × (MB − LOI) ÷ (99.95 − LOI)

It reverses NF using the mass balance instead of the rounded NF. Because NF = (99.95 − LOI) ÷ OXTRUE and MB − LOI = OXTRUE, multiplying by (MB − LOI) ÷ (99.95 − LOI) is exactly dividing by the unrounded NF on DUP and STD rows. On AVG and cargo rows it is applied to averaged values, so it is a close approximation rather than exact.

PQ's CalculationRe-normalise to MB Coll

NF NEW = (MB Coll − LOI) ÷ OXTRUE,   Result NEW = X × WT × H2OFCT × NF NEW

PQ's calculation doesn't remove normalisation. It normalises again, to the product's typical mass balance (MB Coll) instead of 99.95. CCLAS stores the result in the XRF (Template) tab, columns CW to DK: WT Factor, LOI 425, MB, MB Coll, NF NEW and FE NEW … KNO NEW. The tool recalculates it independently from the X values and shows any difference (recalculated − CCLAS).

AVG rows are the mean of their DUP A and DUP B, composite rows (e.g. "A") the mean of their AVG rows, and the CARGO row the mean of its composites, the same way CCLAS builds them. Rows with no children fall back to Reported × (MB Coll − LOI) ÷ (99.95 − LOI).

LOI pendingBefore LOI drops in

Pending = X × WT × H2OFCT   (NF = 1)  →  Final = Pending × NF

While the LOI result is still outstanding, CCLAS can't work out NF (it needs LOI), so the XRF result shows without normalisation: the raw value corrected to a dry basis. When LOI drops in, NF = (99.95 − LOI) ÷ OXTRUE is applied and the result moves to the final reported value. The change is Pending × (NF − 1): it goes up if MB is below 99.95 and down if MB is above it.

Background and findings

Why this tool exists

CCLAS normalises every XRF result so that oxides + LOI = 99.95. This tool recovers the un-normalised (dry-basis) results, and reproduces PQ's calculation that re-normalises to each product's typical mass balance (MB Coll) instead.

Where the formulas come from

The CCLAS normalisation chain was traced from PQ's working formulas (row 41 of the XRF (Template) tab in FI_CCLAS_New.xlsx). It was then confirmed on the full CCLAS export of job 35447 JMBF (3035011.txt): OXIDES, NF, MB and the reported Fe all recalculate to within rounding on every DUP and STD row.

Findings on job 35447 JMBF

  • MB method is within about 0.006 Fe of the exact raw value (X × H2OFCT; largest gap 0.0064) and works on every row. On the four AVG rows it is within 0.0053 Fe of the mean of their DUPs' raw Fe.
  • Reported ÷ NF is within about 0.033 Fe (largest gap 0.0334). The gap is because CCLAS shows NF to 3 dp.
  • AVG and cargo rows have no X values and show NF = 1.000. Their MB is the average of their DUPs' MB (e.g. A1 AVG: (100.007 + 99.916) ÷ 2 = 99.9615, shown as 99.961).
  • The NEW columns (FE NEW …) are a re-normalisation to MB Coll, not a removal of normalisation. They sit about 0.05–0.10 Fe below the raw values. Averaging AVG/composite/cargo rows from their DUPs, the tool's recalculation of PQ's calculation matches the NEW columns on 157 of 160 values on 35447 JMBF and on 35023 MACF (XRF template). The rest differ by 0.01 (X rounding).

MB Coll

MB Coll is one fixed value per material and isn't calculated from the job itself. None of the job's means, medians or weightings (tonnage, bag weight) reproduce it.

Methods tried and dropped

  • Analytical total (V × S ÷ 100, S from the sum of oxides): S from reported values is always about 100, so it changes nothing. CCLAS also normalises to 99.95 with LOI held fixed, not to 100.
  • LOI basis (V × (100 − LOI) ÷ 100): CCLAS results are already on a dry basis with LOI in the total, so this applied LOI twice.
  • Result × NF: the wrong direction. CCLAS multiplies the raw value by NF.

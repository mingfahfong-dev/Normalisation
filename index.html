Removing normalisation from XRF reporting

About this tool

Paste a CCLAS XRF job and simulate how CCLAS builds the results. Normalisation simulation: recover the un-normalised (dry-basis) results and compare with PQ's re-normalisation to MB Coll. LOI simulation: see results while H2O and LOI are pending, and project the final results before they arrive.

Dotted-underlined terms: hover (or tap) for what they mean. Full list in the glossary on "How it's calculated". Settings for each simulation are under Settings on its tab.

How CCLAS normalises an XRF result

  1. The instrument measures the fused bead and gives the raw X value for each analyte (XFE, XSIO2 …).
  2. It corrects to a dry basis with H2OFCT = 100 ÷ (100 − H2O) (H2O is the residual moisture of the pulp), and for the process weight with the WT Factor (1 for process P).
  3. It adds up the oxides: OXIDES = XFe×1.4297 + XP×2.2914 + XSiO2 + XAl2O3 + XTiO2 + XCaO + XMn×1.3883 + XMgO + XK2O (S and Na2O are not included). OXTRUE = OXIDES × WT × H2OFCT, and the mass balance MB = OXTRUE + LOI.
  4. It scales the oxides so that oxides + LOI = 99.95, holding LOI fixed: NF = (99.95 − LOI) ÷ OXTRUE.
  5. The reported result = X × WT × H2OFCT × NF. AVG and cargo rows are the averages of their DUPs, so they have no X values and show NF = 1.000. Their MB is the average of the DUPs' MB.

Normalisation simulation

Recovering the un-normalised (dry-basis) result from what CCLAS reports, and checking PQ's re-normalisation to MB Coll.

MB methodDefault, best for every row

Un-normalised = Reported × (MB − LOI) ÷ (99.95 − LOI)

It reverses NF using the mass balance instead of the rounded NF. Because NF = (99.95 − LOI) ÷ OXTRUE and MB − LOI = OXTRUE, multiplying by (MB − LOI) ÷ (99.95 − LOI) is exactly dividing by the unrounded NF on DUP and STD rows. On AVG and cargo rows it is applied to averaged values, so it is a close approximation rather than exact.

PQ's CalculationRe-normalise to MB Coll

NF NEW = (MB Coll − LOI) ÷ OXTRUE,   Result NEW = X × WT × H2OFCT × NF NEW

PQ's calculation doesn't remove normalisation. It normalises again, to the product's typical mass balance (MB Coll) instead of 99.95. CCLAS stores the result in the XRF (Template) tab, columns CW to DK: WT Factor, LOI 425, MB, MB Coll, NF NEW and FE NEW … KNO NEW. The tool recalculates it independently from the X values and shows any difference (recalculated − CCLAS).

AVG rows are the mean of their DUP A and DUP B, composite rows (e.g. "A") the mean of their AVG rows, and the CARGO row the mean of its composites, the same way CCLAS builds them. Rows with no children fall back to Reported × (MB Coll − LOI) ÷ (99.95 − LOI).

Scenarios: removing normalisation

The Scenarios view compares three ways of reporting each row. B is the current reported result (the sample's top line). A is the un-normalised result ISO recommends: X × WT × H2OFCT on DUP/STD rows, the MB method on AVG and cargo rows, with MB used as a pass/fail gate (set in Settings, default 99.0–101.0) instead of being forced to 99.95. C is PQ's re-normalisation to MB Coll, which is smoother than 99.95 but still a normalisation.

The cards show how far cargo Fe would move under A and C, the largest single-row change, and how many beads would fail the MB gate. ASCRM rows on line A are flagged against PQ's limits, which were set on normalised data and should be re-derived before switching.

ASCRM expected values

ASCRM rows (STD) can show an Expected line with the certified value (Certificate of Analysis) and the difference from it, and the reported (or projected) values are flagged against PQ's limits (pasted final values, the Reported ÷ NF line, and projected values in the LOI simulation): yellow = outside the warning range (Low–High) but inside the action range, red = outside the action range (Low Low–High High). In the LOI simulation the certified LOI can also drive the projection.

Certified values are from Table 3 of each certificate (SA TR 2.30, 2.32, 2.34 and 2.35, as screenshots in ASCRM_New_Limits.xlsx), for all ten elements and LOI. Si, Al, Ca, Mg, Ti and K are certified as elements and converted to oxides: SiO2 = Si × 2.1390, Al2O3 = Al × 1.8900, CaO = Ca × 1.3992, MgO = Mg × 1.6578, TiO2 = Ti × 1.6680, K2O = K × 1.2045. PQ's warning and action limits (Fe, SiO2, Al2O3, P, LOI) come from Kim's sheet in the same workbook; ASCRM35 has none, so it gets an expected line but no flags or limit lines. With Show PQ limit lines on (Settings), two lines under each ASCRM row list the warning range (Low–High) and action range (Low Low–High High), low value on top and high below; hover a range for all four decimals. Note ASCRM34 Al2O3: certificate 2.249, but Kim's limits are centred on 2.255.

LOI simulation

What CCLAS shows before H2O and LOI arrive, and projecting the final result from an LOI estimate.

LOI pendingBefore LOI drops in

Pending = X × WT  →  Final = Pending × H2OFCT × NF

H2O and LOI come in together, so while they're outstanding CCLAS shows the raw XRF value: H2OFCT = 1.000 and NF = 1.000. When they drop in, two things happen at once: the result is corrected to a dry basis (× H2OFCT, about +0.4 Fe at 0.7% H2O) and normalised (× NF). The change is Pending × (H2OFCT × NF − 1), so Fe almost always goes up.

Confirmed on job 3034098AF, exported before and after LOI: the pending values equal X × WT on all 90 element values of the DUP and STD rows. In that export the AVG, composite and cargo rows did not change, so the tool shows the pending line on DUP and STD rows only.

Needs
X values and WT (DUP and STD rows)
Shows
Pending value, and the change (final − pending) under it

Projected final

Projected = X × (99.95 − LOI estimate) ÷ OXIDES

H2O cancels out: Final = X × WT × H2OFCT × NF and NF = (99.95 − LOI) ÷ (OXIDES × WT × H2OFCT), so WT and H2OFCT divide out and the final result depends only on LOI. With the real LOI this reproduces CCLAS on 85 of 90 values of job 3034098AF (the rest differ by 0.01). The LOI estimate is the FY27 shipping target for the row's product, editable in "LOI estimates for projection" on the Calculator tab.

Each 1% of LOI moves Fe by about −0.64 at 60% Fe. The ± figure uses the FY27 LOI 3SD (0.35 for Fines, 0.30 for Lump), so a 0.35 LOI miss moves Fe about 0.22. ASCRM standards use their certified LOI (ASCRM30 9.24, ASCRM32 2.90, ASCRM34 4.76) with PQ's LOI warning step (about ±0.03 to ±0.06) as the spread. If the data already has LOI, the projection is compared with the actual result, which shows how good the estimate was.

Background and findings

Why this tool exists

CCLAS normalises every XRF result so that oxides + LOI = 99.95. This tool recovers the un-normalised (dry-basis) results, and reproduces PQ's calculation that re-normalises to MB Coll, PQ's rolling 3-month average of MB for the product, instead.

Where the formulas come from

The CCLAS normalisation chain was traced from PQ's working formulas (row 41 of the XRF (Template) tab in FI_CCLAS_New.xlsx). It was then confirmed on the full CCLAS export of job 35447 JMBF (3035011.txt): OXIDES, NF, MB and the reported Fe all recalculate to within rounding on every DUP and STD row.

Findings on job 35447 JMBF

  • MB method is within about 0.006 Fe of the exact raw value (X × H2OFCT; largest gap 0.0064) and works on every row. On the four AVG rows it is within 0.0053 Fe of the mean of their DUPs' raw Fe.
  • Reported ÷ NF is within about 0.033 Fe (largest gap 0.0334). The gap is because CCLAS shows NF to 3 dp.
  • AVG and cargo rows have no X values and show NF = 1.000. Their MB is the average of their DUPs' MB (e.g. A1 AVG: (100.007 + 99.916) ÷ 2 = 99.9615, shown as 99.961).
  • The NEW columns (FE NEW …) are a re-normalisation to MB Coll, not a removal of normalisation. They sit about 0.05–0.10 Fe below the raw values. Averaging AVG/composite/cargo rows from their DUPs, the tool's recalculation of PQ's calculation matches the NEW columns on 157 of 160 values on 35447 JMBF and on 35023 MACF (XRF template). The rest differ by 0.01 (X rounding).

MB Coll

MB Coll is PQ's rolling 3-month average of MB data, supplied by PQ. That is why it can't be reproduced from a single job's own MBs (no mean, median or weighting of the job matches it), and why it moves slowly between jobs of the same product (MACF: 100.042 on 35023, 100.242 on 35215). Re-normalising to it puts each result on the product's recent typical mass balance instead of the fixed 99.95.

Methods tried and dropped

  • Analytical total (V × S ÷ 100, S from the sum of oxides): S from reported values is always about 100, so it changes nothing. CCLAS also normalises to 99.95 with LOI held fixed, not to 100.
  • LOI basis (V × (100 − LOI) ÷ 100): CCLAS results are already on a dry basis with LOI in the total, so this applied LOI twice.
  • Result × NF: the wrong direction. CCLAS multiplies the raw value by NF.
